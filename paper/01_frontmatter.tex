\begin{abstract}
An image can look unchanged even after its hidden message has become unrecoverable. That gap between visual quality and transport validity motivated this study. We first built an explicit, auditable interpretation of a reported contourlet pipeline. We then introduced a separate C0--C3 digital transport that divides the secret into Base and Detail layers, isolates adaptive coefficient allocation and unequal Reed--Solomon protection, and keeps both payload and realised cover--stego PSNR fixed. In C3, Base receives 65 RS(255,127) codewords instead of the reference mixture of 32 strong and 22 weaker blocks. Its nominal correction budget rises from 2,752 to 4,160 symbols (51.2\%), and its share of the protected body rises from 50.0\% to 60.2\%. The locked experiment contained 16 embeddings, 64 mandatory evaluations, and 24 conditional hard-channel checks. Every clean stream decoded correctly. None of the tested JPEG, Gaussian, or salt-and-pepper streams passed the complete integrity check. C3 therefore provides a verified code-level Base-layer advantage, but no end-to-end recovery advantage at the tested operating point. The accompanying evidence chain records both results without substituting plausible output for a failed decode.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} image steganography; contourlet transform; unequal error protection; adaptive embedding; Reed--Solomon coding; reproducibility.

\section{Introduction}

A stego image can be visually convincing and still fail at its only hidden task: carrying a message to the receiver. Visual similarity answers whether embedding damaged the cover. It does not answer whether the message survived compression, noise, quantisation, or an extraction rule that rejects corrupted output.

Our initial goal was to reproduce a recently reported contourlet pipeline \citep{Kumar2026}. That goal changed when the published description did not determine a unique set of filters, directional bands, writable coefficients, or datatype boundaries. Different reasonable choices produced materially different systems. Rather than hide that ambiguity behind one convenient implementation, we made it part of the study: source-reported steps, engineering controls, and an independently specified PDFB interpretation were recorded separately.

The reconstruction also exposed a more general problem. An embedding rule is not yet a digital transport. It says where values are perturbed, but not how the payload identifies itself, which bits deserve more protection, how burst errors are dispersed, or when a recovered image should be withheld because its integrity cannot be established.

We therefore designed a separate C0--C3 architecture. The secret is split into a perceptually dominant Base layer and a Detail layer. One factor changes how transform coordinates are selected; the other redistributes Reed--Solomon protection toward Base. C3 combines both and writes Base before Detail in the higher-ranked coordinate groups. Every variant carries the same 222,360-bit stream and is pushed to the same realised PSNR boundary, so neither a smaller payload nor additional distortion can masquerade as robustness.

The result was informative precisely because it was not uniformly favourable. C3 increased the designed protection of Base, yet none of the attacked streams passed the final integrity check. The paper therefore asks a narrower question than whether one method is simply ``better'': what was strengthened, where did that strength stop, and can another researcher reconstruct the same boundary from the evidence?

\section{Study Contributions}

This work makes four primary contributions.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,label=\arabic*.]
  \item \textbf{Hierarchical Base-layer protection.} C3 gives perceptually dominant information both stronger error-control coding and earlier access to adaptively ranked transform coordinates.
  \item \textbf{Transform-bound adaptation.} Band scores combine energy, variance, coefficient-magnitude entropy, and channel stability estimated only from separate calibration data and tied to the active transform fingerprint.
  \item \textbf{A self-describing validity contract.} The protected stream records the method, ECC layout, dimensions, seeds, payload CRC, and configuration identity. Failed Reed--Solomon or CRC checks do not emit a substitute secret image.
  \item \textbf{A controlled factorial comparison.} C0--C3 isolate adaptive allocation and unequal protection while preserving the payload, transform, image-quality target, pair, and channel realisation.
\end{enumerate}

The main positive result is code-level rather than end-to-end: the nominal Base correction budget rises by 51.2\%, the Base share of the body transport rises to 60.2\%, and 22 formerly weaker Base blocks move from a 32-symbol to a 64-symbol correction limit. The implementation also contributes an explicit PDFB profile, deterministic coefficient maps, codeword-level failure records, content-addressed results, and a tested restart path after a real SIGKILL. Supplementary Notes S1--S6 document those controls.

\section{Related Work and Novelty Positioning}

Contourlet and wavelet steganography use multiscale image structure to balance imperceptibility and recoverability \citep{DoVetterli2005,Cox2007,Fridrich2009}. Earlier contourlet studies introduced cover selection and content-adaptive embedding \citep{SajediJamzad2010,Fakhredanesh2013}. Unequal protection has likewise been explored in steganography and image communication \citep{MaoQin2013,AlmawganiSalleh2011}. The present claim is therefore not that adaptation, UEP, or Reed--Solomon coding is new in isolation.
