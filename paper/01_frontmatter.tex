\begin{abstract}
A visually convincing stego image is only part of a successful hidden communication system: the concealed message must also remain identifiable, decodable, and verifiably intact. This study develops an audited hierarchical transport for contourlet-domain image steganography that gives greater protection to the information most responsible for the visual structure of the secret image. The secret is divided into a perceptually dominant Base layer and a complementary Detail layer. Four controlled configurations, C0--C3, separate adaptive coordinate allocation from unequal Reed--Solomon protection while preserving the same 222,360-bit payload and the same realised cover--stego PSNR target of \(45.0\pm0.1\) dB. C3 combines transform-aware coordinate ranking with stronger Base-layer coding and Base-first placement in the highest-ranked multiscale coordinate groups. This increases the nominal Base correction budget from 2,752 to 4,160 symbols, a gain of 51.2\%, while increasing the Base share of the protected body from 50.0\% to 60.2\%. The implementation also introduces independently writable P3+P4 range coordinates in an explicit 9--7/PKVA PDFB, a self-describing protected header, layered integrity checks, deterministic coefficient maps, and content-addressed execution evidence. All sixteen clean streams decoded successfully, validating the complete transport path. The tested JPEG, Gaussian, and salt-and-pepper conditions remained beyond the complete-payload recovery region of all four configurations. Together, these results establish a verified Base-layer protection advantage and a clear experimental boundary for the next generation of layered, validity-aware steganographic transport.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} image steganography; contourlet transform; unequal error protection; adaptive embedding; Reed--Solomon coding; reproducibility.

\section{Introduction}

Image steganography is often judged by what an observer can see. If the stego image remains visually close to its cover, the embedding stage appears successful. For digital communication, however, visual similarity is only the beginning. The hidden payload must also remain identifiable, decodable, and verifiably intact after compression, noise, quantisation, and the numerical boundaries of the transform implementation.

This distinction motivated the present study. Our starting point was a recently reported contourlet-domain pipeline \citep{Kumar2026}. Reconstructing that pipeline required more than translating a sequence of processing labels into code. The published description left several outcome-determining choices open, including the precise filter configuration, the organisation of directional bands, the definition of writable coordinates, and the datatype transitions surrounding inverse transformation. We therefore converted these choices into an explicit and inspectable system specification while keeping the source reconstruction separate from the new digital transport.

That reconstruction revealed a broader opportunity. A transform-domain embedding rule describes how image coefficients may be perturbed, but it does not by itself provide a complete digital transport. A practical transport must identify its own format, organise the payload, distribute error-control protection, reproduce its coefficient map, and distinguish a verified secret from a plausible but corrupted image.

We therefore developed the separate DIGITAL\_A\_D architecture around one guiding idea: the bits that contribute most strongly to the reconstructed secret should receive the strongest available protection. The four most-significant bits of every secret pixel form a perceptually dominant Base layer, while the four least-significant bits form a complementary Detail layer. This representation preserves the complete secret while allowing protection to follow information importance.

The architecture explores two complementary mechanisms. The first, A, ranks eligible multiscale coordinates using coefficient energy, variance, magnitude entropy, and channel stability measured on separate calibration images. The second, D, redistributes Reed--Solomon protection toward Base. Their combination, C3, gives Base both stronger coding and earlier access to the coordinate groups favoured by the transform-bound calibration policy.

Four controlled configurations make these mechanisms directly observable. C0 provides fixed allocation with symmetric coding. C1 introduces adaptive allocation while retaining symmetric coding. C2 introduces unequal Base/Detail protection while retaining fixed allocation. C3 integrates both mechanisms and uses Base-first placement. Every configuration carries the same 222,360-bit protected stream and is calibrated to the same realised cover--stego PSNR target of \(45.0\pm0.1\) dB. Consequently, no method can gain an apparent advantage by carrying less information or by introducing more visible distortion.

C3 uses the fixed transport budget more selectively. Base receives 65 RS(255,127) codewords instead of the reference mixture of 32 RS(255,127) and 22 RS(255,191) blocks. This increases its nominal correction budget from 2,752 to 4,160 erroneous symbols, a gain of 51.2\%. Its share of the protected body rises from 50.0\% to 60.2\%, and 22 Base blocks move from a 32-symbol to a 64-symbol correction limit.

A second contribution emerged from the transform itself. The number of entries in a directional representation does not automatically equal the number of independently writable degrees of freedom. We therefore constructed and verified an explicit multiscale coordinate space over the valid P3+P4 range of a locked 9--7/PKVA PDFB implementation. The resulting pool provides sufficient independently writable capacity for the complete fixed payload while retaining deterministic analysis, embedding, reconstruction, and extraction maps.

The completed experiment produced 16 embedding objects, 64 core evaluations, and 24 predeclared hard-channel evaluations. All sixteen clean streams decoded successfully. This validates the complete path across payload construction, error-control coding, coordinate selection, PDFB embedding, inverse transformation, uint8 quantisation, semi-blind extraction, and integrity checking.

The tested JPEG, Gaussian, and salt-and-pepper conditions remained beyond the complete-payload recovery region of all four configurations. This result provides a useful threshold observation rather than an ambiguous output: C3 established a measurable Base-layer protection advantage, while the selected channel severities identified where additional architectural development is required.

\section{Study Contributions}

This work makes six connected contributions.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,label=\arabic*.]
  \item \textbf{Perceptually informed payload organisation.} The secret is divided into Base and Detail according to the contribution of each bit plane to the reconstructed pixel value.
  \item \textbf{Hierarchical Base-layer protection.} C3 reallocates the fixed Reed--Solomon budget toward Base, increasing its nominal correction capacity by 51.2\% without reducing the payload or increasing the available coefficient capacity.
  \item \textbf{Joint coding and placement prioritisation.} Base receives both stronger codewords and earlier access to higher-ranked multiscale coordinates.
  \item \textbf{Transform-bound adaptive allocation.} Eligible coordinate groups are ranked using a robust combination of energy, variance, magnitude entropy, and calibration-derived stability tied to the active transform fingerprint.
  \item \textbf{An independently writable PDFB coordinate space.} The study constructs and verifies sufficient independent P3+P4 range coordinates within a locked 9--7/PKVA PDFB interpretation.
  \item \textbf{Validity-aware and interruption-resilient research transport.} A self-describing protected header, layered integrity checks, content-addressed objects, and a tested restart path preserve both successful recovery and structured evidence from unsuccessful attempts.
\end{enumerate}

The main measured advance is the verified strengthening of the Base layer. Its protected share rises from 50.0\% to 60.2\%, its nominal correction budget rises from 2,752 to 4,160 symbols, and every formerly weaker Base block is replaced with a stronger RS(255,127) block. The implementation further contributes explicit transform identity, deterministic coefficient maps, codeword-level records, and restart-safe research execution.

\section{Related Work and Novelty Positioning}

Transform-domain steganography uses the spatial and frequency structure of an image to balance embedding capacity, visual quality, and payload recovery \citep{DoVetterli2005,Cox2007,Fridrich2009}. Contourlet representations are particularly attractive because their multiscale and directional components can describe edges and image activity more flexibly than a single-scale transform.

Earlier contourlet studies demonstrated the value of content-aware cover selection, region selection, and adaptive embedding \citep{SajediJamzad2010,Fakhredanesh2013}. Unequal error protection has also been applied to embedded image information and layered image communication \citep{MaoQin2013,AlmawganiSalleh2011}. More recent work has combined adaptive embedding-domain selection with adaptive error correction for lossy JPEG channels \citep{Duan2023}. The contribution of the present study is therefore not any one component in isolation. Its value lies in integrating semantic Base/Detail organisation, fixed-budget unequal coding, transform-bound adaptive placement, independently writable PDFB coordinates, fixed payload and quality controls, self-describing validity, and interruption-resilient provenance.
