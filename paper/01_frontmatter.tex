\begin{abstract}
Reproducible image steganography depends on more than an embedding transform: payload framing, coefficient selection, error control, quantisation, channel definition, and failed-decode handling can all change the result. We developed an auditable contourlet reconstruction and a separate hierarchical digital transport that divides a secret into Base and Detail layers, combines unequal Reed--Solomon protection with transform-bound adaptive allocation, and evaluates all variants under one fixed 222,360-bit payload and a common \SI{45.0\pm0.1}{dB} cover--stego PSNR constraint. In C3, Base receives 65 RS(255,127) codewords instead of the reference mixture of 32 strong and 22 weaker blocks. Its nominal symbol-correction budget therefore rises from 2,752 to 4,160 symbols (51.2\%), while its share of the body transport rises from 50.0\% to 60.2\%. The locked study comprised 16 embeddings, 64 mandatory evaluations, and 24 conditional hard-channel checks. All clean cases decoded exactly. Under the tested JPEG, Gaussian, and salt-and-pepper channels, however, no method achieved valid complete recovery. The study thus demonstrates a verified code-level Base-layer advantage, not an end-to-end robustness advantage at the tested operating point, while providing a failure-aware and restart-safe evidence chain.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} image steganography; contourlet transform; unequal error protection; adaptive embedding; Reed--Solomon coding; reproducibility.

\section{Introduction}

Image steganography asks a demanding question: can information be hidden without visibly damaging its carrier and still be recovered after the carrier has passed through a real digital channel? A visually convincing stego image answers only the first half. Recovery also depends on the payload contract, writable transform coordinates, quantisation, channel implementation, and the rule used to distinguish valid output from a plausible but corrupted reconstruction.

This dependence is especially important in transform-domain methods. Naming a contourlet transform does not uniquely determine the filters, directional schedule, boundary handling, coefficient ordering, or eligible subbands. Two implementations can therefore share the same verbal description while exposing different capacities and failure modes. The source study motivating this work combines geometric transformations of the secret with contourlet-domain embedding \citep{Kumar2026}, but several outcome-determining details remain insufficient to identify one author-equivalent executable pipeline.

We treated that ambiguity as evidence rather than filling it with favourable assumptions. Source-reported behaviour, engineering controls, and an independently specified PDFB interpretation were kept separate. This reconstruction also exposed a broader limitation: an embedding rule alone is not a complete digital transport. It does not specify how important bits are prioritised, how errors are corrected, how the stream identifies its own configuration, or how invalid decodes are reported.

We therefore designed a separate C0--C3 transport architecture. The secret is divided into a perceptually dominant Base layer and a Detail layer. Unequal protection assigns stronger Reed--Solomon codes to Base, while adaptive allocation ranks eligible transform bands using image statistics and channel stability learned from separate calibration data. C3 combines both mechanisms and places Base before Detail in higher-scoring coordinate groups. All methods carry the same 222,360-bit protected stream and are driven to the same realised PSNR boundary, preventing payload reduction or additional distortion from masquerading as robustness.

The experiment produced a deliberately bounded result. C3 is demonstrably stronger at the Base-layer coding level: its nominal correction budget increases by 51.2\%, its protected transport share rises to 60.2\%, and the formerly weak Base blocks double their correction limit. Nevertheless, every tested method failed the strict complete-payload criterion under the selected medium and hard channels. The scientific contribution is therefore twofold: a verified hierarchical protection architecture and an evaluation framework that preserves the difference between designed strength, partial preservation, and valid end-to-end recovery.

\section{Contributions}

The paper makes four primary scientific contributions.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,label=\arabic*.]
  \item \textbf{Hierarchical Base-layer protection.} C3 protects perceptually dominant information through both stronger error-control coding and earlier placement in adaptively ranked transform coordinates.
  \item \textbf{Transform-bound adaptation.} Eligible bands are scored from energy, variance, coefficient-magnitude entropy, and channel stability estimated only from separate calibration data and bound to the transform fingerprint.
  \item \textbf{A self-describing, failure-aware transport.} A protected header binds the stream to its method, ECC layout, dimensions, seeds, payload CRC, and configuration; invalid Reed--Solomon or CRC outcomes never generate a fabricated recovered image.
  \item \textbf{A controlled factorial evaluation.} C0--C3 isolate adaptive allocation and unequal protection under one payload, transform, quality target, image pairing, and channel realisation.
\end{enumerate}

The strongest verified positive findings are numerical: the Base correction budget increases from 2,752 to 4,160 symbols, the Base transport share increases from 50.0\% to 60.2\%, 22 formerly weaker Base blocks move from a 32-symbol to a 64-symbol correction limit, and all 16 clean embeddings decode exactly. Supporting reproducibility contributions include an explicit PDFB profile, deterministic context-bound randomisation, a hashable coefficient map, codeword-level failure telemetry, content-addressed result objects, and tested restart after a real SIGKILL. Implementation details are provided in Supplementary Notes S1--S5.

\section{Related Work and Novelty Positioning}

Contourlet and wavelet steganography use multiscale structure to balance imperceptibility and recoverability \citep{DoVetterli2005,Cox2007,Fridrich2009}. Earlier contourlet studies introduced cover selection and adaptive embedding \citep{SajediJamzad2010,Fakhredanesh2013}; unequal protection has also been explored in steganographic and image-communication systems \citep{MaoQin2013,AlmawganiSalleh2011}. These precedents mean that adaptation, UEP, and Reed--Solomon coding are not claimed as isolated firsts.

The novelty lies in their controlled integration: transform-bound calibration, joint coding-and-coordinate prioritisation, a fixed-payload and fixed-quality factorial comparison, strict failure-aware validity, and an interruption-resilient provenance chain.
