\section{Discussion}

The central result requires a distinction among three levels of evidence. C3 has stronger \emph{designed} Base protection because its code structure and correction limits are verified. Layer BER and corrected-symbol records can measure \emph{exercised} correction. Valid complete recovery is a stricter outcome requiring a decodable header, both layers, and a matching payload CRC. The present experiment supports the first level, preserves evidence for the second, and does not support an end-to-end advantage under the evaluated channels.

Several factors can explain why the verified protection increase did not cross the final threshold. The \SI{45}{dB} quality constraint limits coefficient displacement; the 222,360-bit transport occupies a substantial coordinate pool; one critical header or layer-codeword failure invalidates complete recovery; and unequal protection intentionally transfers redundancy away from Detail. The selected medium conditions may also lie beyond the narrow transition region in which a 51.2\% increase changes the binary validity outcome.

The negative result remains scientifically useful because the protocol was not altered after observation. Payload size, PSNR, coefficient range, attacks, seeds, and image pairs were not repeatedly changed until a favourable table appeared. The completed experiment therefore identifies a precise boundary: clean transport validity was achieved, Base protection was structurally increased, but the tested channels remained beyond complete recovery for all configurations.

Reproducibility strengthens this interpretation. Each row is linked to one pair, method, transform fingerprint, attack realisation, embedding object, and evaluation object. A future threshold study can therefore add evidence without rewriting the completed run.

\section{Limitations and Future Work}

The study uses four traceability cases and supports case-bounded, not population-level, inference. The PDFB profile is explicit but not author-equivalent; extraction is semi-blind; geometric attacks and steganalysis are outside the final factorial evidence; and deterministic scrambling is not cryptographic encryption. The strict complete-payload rule can also hide useful partial Base preservation.

The next experiment should preregister finer channel increments between clean operation and the failed medium settings. It should report header validity, Base and Detail codeword failures, corrected-symbol distributions, layer BER, Base-only integrity, payload CRC, and \(\eur\). A separately validated Base CRC could additionally test scalable low-fidelity reconstruction when Detail is lost. Direct comparison with the source article should wait until transform, payload, pairing, quantisation, extraction, attacks, and metrics are harmonised.

\section{Conclusion}

We transformed an underspecified contourlet description into an auditable digital-transport experiment. The resulting C3 architecture combines hierarchical Base/Detail coding, transform-bound adaptation, fixed-payload and fixed-quality comparison, self-describing failure-aware decoding, and restart-safe provenance.

C3 increases the Base transport share from 50.0\% to 60.2\%, increases its nominal correction budget by 51.2\%, doubles the correction limit of the formerly weak Base blocks, and places Base earlier in higher-scoring coordinates. These verified advantages did not yield valid complete recovery under the locked JPEG, Gaussian, and salt-and-pepper channels. The evidence therefore supports stronger Base-layer protection and a more auditable system, while locating the tested operating point beyond the end-to-end recovery threshold.
